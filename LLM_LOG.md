# LLM Usage Log

This log covers AI assistance with the exact-payment project, from the Solution DSL to the Grover circuit and validation. It records the explanations and implementation help we used, and the checks we ran. ChatGPT (GPT-6.1) is the model I reported. Part II project work documented here used Codex.

| 1 | ChatGPT (GPT-6.1) | I | Model exact payment as a linear constraint problem | Asked for help expressing exact payment as a linear constraint and representing the 17-cent case in the Solution DSL. | I used one binary variable per coin and a linear equality for the target amount. The metamodel separates variables, terms, expressions and constraints, and can also represent a limit on the number of coins. The metamodel, example and diagrams are in `837d4f2`. | In the later review, we checked the example against the wallet data. The coefficients are 2, 5, 10 and 20, and selecting the first three coins gives 17 cents. |
| 2 | ChatGPT (GPT-6.1) | I and II | Understand how the two DSLs fit into the assignment | Compare the project with the assignment and explain how the payment model, constraint model and circuit fit together. | The walkthrough helped me explain the difference between the wallet and payment objects in the Problem DSL and the variables and constraints in the Solution DSL. I used the 17-cent example to follow the same input through T1, T2 and T3. | The initial check had covered T1. Reading the full brief showed that Part II also required circuit generation, execution, domain-level results and validation against a classical method. |
| 3 | ChatGPT (GPT-6.1) | II | Make T1 work for different wallets and optional coin limits | Remove the temporary coin limit, read the available coins from the selected payment’s wallet, and verify the result on the existing BESSER models. | In the prototype, T1 reads the payment’s wallet and adds the coin-count inequality only when maxPieces is set. The original example produced 11 objects, and the test with a limit of three coins produced 18. | The two-wallet test exposed that BESSER can return a single linked coin as an object rather than a collection. We handled both forms and checked that a payment only uses coins from its own wallet. |
| 4 | ChatGPT (GPT-6.1) | II | Work out what the Grover configuration needs to represent | Asked how shots, the search-space size and the number of valid subsets affect a Grover run. | I kept 1,024 shots in the default configuration and left the round count optional. The pipeline supplies the classical solution count for the automatic round calculation. The configuration files are in `eaff9ff`. | Using Ana’s example helped separate shots from rounds. One valid subset out of 16 gives three Grover rounds. The 1,024 shots are repetitions of the resulting circuit. |
| 5 | ChatGPT (GPT-6.1) | II | Choose and verify the reversible arithmetic for T2 | Asked how QFT addition and controlled-X addition differ for our constraints, and how to tell whether either oracle marks the right candidates. | We chose controlled-X addition for the final version. T2 derives the register widths, computes the constraints, marks valid choices and reverses the computation before diffusion. The final T2 is in `efb1e33`. | We set aside the QFT prototype for submission after comparing how the two pipelines validate the arithmetic. The chosen pipeline checks every candidate input in its five examples. Since shot counts differ, we compared each result with its classical baseline and theoretical probability. |
| 6 | ChatGPT (GPT-6.1) | II | Generate runnable Qiskit and interpret the measured bits | Connect the BESSER circuit to the Qiskit generator and trace the measured bits back to the original coins. | The prototype used BESSER’s gate mapping and a template that decoded the coins in the generated script. The final version calls BESSER’s generator directly and decodes the results in the pipeline. | The installed BESSER package was missing its Qiskit template. For local testing, we supplied the template from the required fork and ran the generated scripts on Aer. Ana’s measured bits decoded to the 2-, 5- and 10-cent coins. |
| 7 | ChatGPT (GPT-6.1) | II | Check that the full chain gives the expected payments | Asked how to check that the simulator returns the right coins and why a measured success rate can differ from the Grover prediction. | The prototype passed its eight automated tests and ran four examples. We also ran all five examples in the chosen version, including one with several solutions and one with none. They agreed with the classical baseline. | A missing valid measurement alone cannot establish that a payment is impossible. For the no-solution example, we used the exhaustive classical result to establish that no valid subset exists. |
| 8 | ChatGPT (GPT-6.1) | II | Explain how the model determines the qubit count | Asked for a walkthrough of the circuit layout for Ana’s 17-cent payment, including the extra qubits used for arithmetic and marking. | I used the prototype to work through the register sizes. Four coins require four choice qubits. The expression ranges from −17 to 20, which needs six signed arithmetic bits, and the equality needs one flag. | We checked the same payment in both implementations. The prototype used 11 qubits. The final circuit used 12 because it has a separate phase qubit. Both found the same payment in three Grover rounds. |
